New features

5 shipped

  • Adjustable AI cleanup, from a light touch to a full polish

    Pick how much the AI reshapes your words — None, Low, Medium, or High — and OpenWhisp always keeps your raw transcript so you can revert to exactly what you said in one click.

    How to use
    MAK-35#121
    Landing next

    Choose a cleanup level in Settings; revert to your exact words anytime.

    1. Open Settings › Cleanup and pick a level: None (verbatim), Low (fixes caps, punctuation, spelling), Medium (also removes fillers and lightly rephrases), or High (also resolves spoken self-corrections like “3, no wait, 4” → “4”).
    2. Dictate as usual — the level you chose is applied automatically.
    3. In History or the overlay, hit “revert to original” to swap the cleaned text back to your raw words.

    The four levels and the raw-transcript revert are built and tested. The Settings dial and the revert button are landing next — today the older on/off “Improve every dictation” toggle is still what's live.

  • Voice editing — “scratch that”, “delete last sentence”, “undo”

    Fix a misspoken phrase without deleting and re-dictating. A deterministic, offline command layer edits the in-progress text before it's committed. No AI required.

    How to use
    MAK-19#117
    Landing next

    Mid-dictation, just say the command as its own phrase.

    1. Start dictation as usual (hold Fn/Globe, or Control+Space).
    2. Pause and say a command on its own — it must be the whole phrase, so ordinary speech like “scratch the surface” is never mistaken for a command.
    3. Release to insert the edited text.

    Say

    • “scratch that” — drop the last thing you said
    • “delete last word” / “delete last sentence”
    • “new paragraph” / “new line”
    • “undo” — reverse the last edit

    The command parser (case- and punctuation-tolerant, including … and curly quotes) is shipped and tested. Connecting it to live dictation is the next step — until then, these phrases are typed literally rather than acted on.

  • A self-learning dictionary that tunes itself

    When you type over a transcription to correct it, OpenWhisp notices the fix and proposes it as a vocabulary term — learning your jargon on your machine, never on someone's server.

    How to use
    MAK-41#120
    Landing next

    Just fix a mistyped word once; OpenWhisp offers to remember it.

    1. Dictate; OpenWhisp inserts the text.
    2. Type over a single misheard word to correct it (e.g. “kubernetis” → “kubernetes”).
    3. OpenWhisp proposes that fix as a vocabulary rule — accept it, and every future transcription auto-corrects that mishearing.
    4. Star your most-used rules to keep them at the top of the Substitutions list.

    The detection logic (only proposes clean single-word fixes, never whole rewrites) and the star/frequency model are shipped and tested. The watcher that captures your corrections and the star/sort editor are the next step — for now, add terms manually in Settings › Cleanup › Substitutions.

  • Richer local formatting — numbers, lists, and markdown

    Opt-in rules turn spoken structure into clean text: “bullet…” into a list, “twenty twenty-six” into 2026, “bold that” into markdown — all deterministic, all off by default so nothing surprises you.

    How to use
    MAK-20#119
    Landing next

    Turn on the rule groups you want, then just speak the structure.

    1. In Settings › Smart Formatting, enable the groups you want (they're independent and off by default).
    2. Dictate naturally — the rules rewrite structure locally, on-device, no LLM.

    Say

    • “five dollars” → $5,  “ten cents” → 10¢
    • “twenty twenty six” → 2026  (spoken times like “ten thirty” are left alone)
    • “bullet buy milk” → - buy milk,  “number one …” → 1. …
    • “heading intro” → # intro,  “bold ship it” → **ship it**

    The rules are shipped, tested, and deliberately off by default so they never touch ordinary prose. The Settings toggles to switch each group on are landing next.

  • Send dictations beyond the focused app

    Foundational plumbing for routing a transcript somewhere other than the app you're in — a file, a Shortcut, a webhook. Built to fail open: if a target can't take it, your text still lands where it always did. Never dropped.

    How to use
    MAK-11#122
    Landing next

    Route a dictation to a file, Shortcut, or webhook — chosen per app.

    1. In Settings, pick an output target for an app or profile (e.g. your notes app → a Markdown file).
    2. Dictate into that app — the text goes to the target instead of being typed in.
    3. Everything else keeps typing into the focused app, exactly as before.

    The routing protocol and its fail-open safety net (if a target fails, your words still land in the focused app — never dropped) are shipped and tested. The Settings picker and the actual file / Shortcut / webhook destinations are the next tickets (MAK-12/13/14).

Fixes

6 shipped

  • Silence no longer errors on one backend but not the other

    Dictating nothing now quietly produces nothing on both whisper backends — no more stray error toast depending on which engine you'd picked in Advanced settings.

    MAK-23#123

  • OpenWhisp can't accidentally kill your other whisper servers

    The engine now checks a process is one it actually launched — by full path, not just name — before signaling it, so your own Homebrew or hand-built whisper-server is safe. Plus a fix for a startup port race.

    MAK-27MAK-28#118

  • A data race in Apple Speech dictation, closed

    Session state is now confined to a single context, so a late recognition callback can never leak a partial from one dictation into the next.

    MAK-29#116

  • Agent rate limiting counts sessions on one honest clock

    The per-client session cap could be bypassed by a long-running session straddling the window edge. It now measures membership and expiry on the same clock, so the cap holds.

    MAK-31#112

  • Post-processing scripts clean up after themselves

    Timed-out post-processing scripts now reap reliably — honest about best-effort process-group isolation, with a guarded kill that can never signal OpenWhisp's own processes.

    MAK-30#115

  • The audio-device watcher tears down cleanly

    The CoreAudio listener that notices when you plug in a mic now removes itself symmetrically — no lingering, unremovable observer.

    MAK-33#114

Under the hood

3 shipped

  • One place for text transforms

    The dictation pipeline now runs through a single, ordered post-processing chain — the groundwork that lets new transforms and output targets drop in cleanly, with behavior verified byte-for-byte against the old path.

    MAK-15#124

  • Corrupt settings files can't take the app down

    All five on-device data stores now share one hardened loader: a bad file is quarantined and the app carries on with sane defaults, instead of a fix living in only one of them.

    MAK-22#113

  • End-to-end audio testing, so features stay working

    A full regression suite now feeds pre-recorded audio through the whole pipeline — the reason "757 tests passing" is more than a number. Every feature above ships with coverage.

    E2E#107–110
Why the ticket numbers? OpenWhisp is local-first and hackable — the receipts are part of the point. Every change here traces to a public issue and a reviewed pull request you can read, run, and fork. Nothing shipped that you can't inspect.
